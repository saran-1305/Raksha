Minute 2: Multi-Sensor Ingestion (How Data Fuels Relocation)

Overview EO Studio
SCREEN ACTION: Open Overview Command Center. Click "Devgram (Chamoli)" preset. Show the live map updating, then click "Satellite Telemetry" tab or open EO Studio.

"RAKSHA does not rely on a single data feed. Real disaster zones are choked with heavy cloud cover, rain, and severed infrastructure. We synthesize four complementary satellite systems and terrain models:"

  • 1. Sentinel-1 SAR Radar (C-Band): Penetrates storm clouds and night darkness to map floodwater backscatter drop and mudflow boundaries.
  • 2. Sentinel-2 Multi-Spectral Optical: 10-meter STAC imagery providing vegetation health (NDVI) and environmental baselines.
  • 3. ISRO Cartosat-3 (0.28m Sub-Meter PAN): Audits open ground (>20,000 m²), ensures building density is under 25%, and verifies a 45m radius cleared helipad.
  • 4. ISRO Bhuvan & NRSC 1:50,000 Thematic Anchor: Provides authoritative land-use classification (LULC), institutional land tenure, and 10-year historical disaster zonation.
  • 5. NASA SRTM 30m DEM: Generates continuous slope angles and topographic transect profiles between the disaster origin and candidate sanctuaries.

Minute 3: The Decision Engine (Dynamic AOI, Vetoes & Sphere Sizing)

View Map Layers
SCREEN ACTION: Point to the map: Red Dynamic AOI polygon, 5km/10km distance rings, Red ✕ (vetoed sites), Green # (passed sites), and the SRTM DEM Elevation Transect chart.

"Here is how RAKSHA converts raw satellite feeds into life-saving decisions:

First, we reject static circular hazard zones. Using precipitation accumulation and terrain slope vectors, RAKSHA computes a Dynamic Anisotropic Hazard AOI along the true hydrological runoff corridor.

Second, we enforce Zero-Tolerance Safety Vetoes. In RAKSHA, an unsafe site is eliminated outright—never merely penalized in score. If a facility sits on a slope over 14 degrees, inside an active inundation zone, or within 50 meters of drainage corridors, it receives an instant hard veto. You can see Site D—the Upper Alaknanda riverbed—eliminated with zero tolerance.

Third, for every passing site, RAKSHA enforces Sphere Humanitarian Standards (2018): 45 square meters per person total settlement space and 3.5 square meters covered living space. We don’t just say 'this school is safe'—we calculate exactly how many of the 6,840 displaced citizens it can legally and humanely absorb."

Jury Q&A Defense Quick-Cards (Top 5 Questions)

Q1: "What if monsoon clouds block optical imagery?" "That is precisely why we ingest Sentinel-1 C-Band SAR radar. SAR uses active microwave signals (5.4 GHz) that penetrate through torrential rain, cloud cover, and nighttime smoke, detecting floodwater backscatter drop regardless of weather."
Q2: "How do you prevent shelter overcrowding?" "We compute capacity strictly using Sphere Humanitarian Standards (2018): 45 m² total settlement space and 3.5 m² covered shelter space per capita. Greedy multi-site allocation spreads excess populations across secondary rings."
Q3: "Can a high-scoring site be chosen on a steep slope?" "No. RAKSHA enforces Zero-Tolerance Hard Vetoes. Safety criteria are boolean gatekeepers, not weighted scores. If slope exceeds 14° or active inundation is detected, the site score is nullified to 0 and eliminated."
Q4: "Why include Cartosat-3 and Bhuvan?" "Global feeds are 10m to 30m resolution. ISRO Cartosat-3 gives 0.28m sub-meter clarity to inspect ingress roads and helipads, while ISRO Bhuvan provides authoritative Indian cadastral tenure and 1:50k LULC required for District Magistrate requisition."
